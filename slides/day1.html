Reading a quasi-experiment

A quasi-experiment is as strong as the assumption you can defend

The fee line at 10 square metres

Stalls of 9.9 and 10.0 square metres are alike, so the gap in renewal is the fee. If nothing else changes at the line, and if holders cannot pick their side of it.

Online booking in six branches

Before you propose a test

Some questions should not be answered with an experiment

  • The benefit is already known. Withholding something proven from half of the citizens is not acceptable.
  • No decision depends on it. If the office will adopt the change whatever the result, do not test it.
  • There are too few units. Three regions cannot be randomised into anything a test can read.
  • The harm cannot be undone. A test needs a guardrail that can stop it in time.
At Mashraq every design answers four questions. Who is in each arm? Why is withholding acceptable? Which data are used? Which guardrail stops the test if a group is harmed?

These four questions come back in sessions 2 and 4 and are items C11 and C12 of the reviewer's checklist. The reminder passes the first test easily: the control arm keeps the service it has today and nobody knows yet whether the reminder helps.

Sahar's question. "Would a reminder seven days before a permit expires get more holders to renew on time?" Today a holder gets one notice, 30 days before expiry, and nothing after it.

Ask the room to turn the question into a decision before showing the next slides. A good answer has a number in it. "If the reminder helps we adopt it" is not yet a rule: helps by how much?

One number in a named population

The estimand says exactly which number the test estimates

  • Hypothesis: holders who get the reminder renew on time more often than holders who get only the standard notice.
  • Estimand: the difference in the share renewing on time, reminder minus control, among holders sent a notice in the test window.
  • It names the metric, the two arms, the direction of the subtraction and the population.

What it leaves out on purpose

Other permit offices. Other seasons. Holders who got no notice in the window. The result will be about the holders in the test; everything else is an argument you make afterwards, in words.

DS-201 used the word estimand for a number in a population. Here it is a difference between two arms. Writing it down prevents the later switch from "share renewing on time" to "days until renewal" or "renewals per notice".

What must not get worse

A guardrail has a metric, a threshold and an action

Complaint rate

Threshold: the reminder arm exceeds control by more than 0.5 points.
Action: stop the test and review the wording.

On-time renewal in each group

Groups: channel (text message, e-mail) and holder type.
Threshold: a group falls more than 3 points below control.
Action: stop the reminder for that group and tell the data protection officer.

An average can hide a harmed group. The second guardrail is how the design applies fairness: it names the groups before anyone looks.

The course's responsible-use line: every design states which guardrail stops the test if a group is harmed. The SDAIA Principles of AI Ethics are the reference the course cites for fairness and for humanity; this slide does not quote them. "We will keep an eye on complaints" is the version to reject: no threshold, no action.

Written before the first notice goes out

The stop rule fixes the end and says what may end the test sooner

  • The test runs 28 days and ends on the planned date.
  • One analysis of the primary metric, at the end.
  • One safety look on day 14 reads the guardrails only.
  • No early stop for a good result.

Who is in each arm

Control: the standard notice, as today. Reminder: the same notice plus one message seven days before expiry. Nobody loses a service; the reminder is untested.

What goes wrong. "We stop when the result is significant." Day 3 counts how often that rule declares a win when nothing differs.

A stop rule has an asymmetry that surprises people: harm may stop a test early, success may not. Stopping for harm protects holders. Stopping for success protects nobody and inflates false wins. Methods that allow planned early looks exist and belong to the next course, DS-302.

Two units that must agree

The unit of randomisation is whoever the coin is tossed for

  • Unit of randomisation: the thing that lands in an arm as a whole: a holder, an applicant, a branch.
  • Unit of analysis: the thing you count when you compute the result.
  • They should be the same. When the analysis counts something smaller, the test looks larger than it is.

The log tempts you

Logs have one row per notice, per page view, per visit. Rows are what systems record. They are rarely what a person experiences as "getting the new version".

How the coin is tossed

Simple, stratified and cluster assignment answer different needs

Simple

One coin per unit. Right when units are many and independent: 60,000 holders.

Stratified

Sort units into groups that matter (channel, baseline level), then toss within each group. The arms match on that trait by construction. Worth it when units are few.

Cluster

Toss for a whole group: a branch and everyone who visits it. Needed when people in a group affect each other. It costs power: Day 2 measures how much.

Whichever you choose, the coin is seeded and recorded. Alternating days, odd and even keys chosen by hand, or "the first half of the list" are not coins.

Stratified is also called blocked; pairing is its smallest form. Lab 2.3 pairs branches by baseline rate and regains much of the power that cluster assignment loses. A hash of the unit key with a seed is the usual way to toss a coin that gives the same answer every time the unit appears.

When units touch each other

If one unit's treatment reaches another, the unit is too small

Inside a branch

The counter change is a triage desk at the entrance. Visitors share one queue and one team. Sending every second visitor to the desk changes the wait of the others too. The visitor cannot be the unit.

Between branches

B03 and B04 share counter staff, as do B11 and B12, B19 and B20. A clerk carries the new routine next door, and staff moved to the desk are missing there. Each pair is one unit.

24 branches, 21 staff pools. The unit of the counter test is the staff pool.

Interference means that a unit's outcome depends on what other units received. Two effects follow: control units are no longer untreated, and the comparison no longer estimates what would happen if everyone got the change. Marketplaces and shared resources make this much harder; DS-302 has two sessions on it.

The reason for the page

A pre-registration removes the choices you would make after seeing the data

  • Which metric leads the report? Which holders are excluded? When does the test stop? Which subgroup is shown?
  • Each choice is reasonable. Made after the data are in, each one leans towards the result you hoped for.
  • Nobody has to cheat. An honest analyst who chooses late still finds too many wins.

What the page is

One page, dated, written before the first unit is assigned, that fixes the decision, the unit, the metrics, the size, the stop rule and the analysis. It can be amended, with a date and a reason, before the test starts. Not after.

The idea is often called the garden of forking paths: many defensible analyses, one of which will look good. Pre-registration is used in clinical trials, in social science and by public-sector experiment teams. Sources: Center for Open Science, Preregistration; Haynes and others (2012), Test, Learn, Adapt.

The reviewer's checklist

One page, twelve items

  • C1 Decision: the action for each result
  • C2 Hypothesis: change, metric, direction
  • C3 Unit: nobody in both arms
  • C4 Primary metric: exactly one, with its window
  • C5 Minimum effect: a number with its reason
  • C6 Guardrails: metric, threshold, action
  • C7 Stop rule: a fixed end, no early stop for success
  • C8 Size and duration: from the minimum effect; whole weeks and the month-end
  • C9 Analysis plan: test, interval, unit, as assigned
  • C10 Checks: sample ratio and balance, before the result
  • C11 Arms and fairness: who, why withholding is acceptable, the group guardrail
  • C12 Data: fields, pseudonymised keys, aggregate reporting

The same list is the file checklist.md in the labs folder, the follow-up task and the first criterion of the capstone rubric. Sessions 2 and 3 wrote C1 to C7 and C11. This session adds C8 to C10 and C12. C8 is provisional today: Day 2 computes it.

C9

The analysis plan is one paragraph that leaves you no choice later

The paragraph

"A two-proportion z-test and a 95% interval for the difference in on-time renewal, with holders as the unit, analysed as assigned whether or not the reminder was delivered. Subgroups by channel and by prior lateness are exploratory and are reported as such."

  • The test and the interval are named, so they cannot be picked afterwards.
  • The unit of analysis is the unit of randomisation.
  • As assigned: every holder counts in the arm the coin chose.
  • Subgroups are named now and labelled exploratory.

"As assigned" is often called intention to treat. Explain it by its failure: if holders whom the reminder did not reach are dropped, they are dropped from one arm only, and those holders are not a random handful. Day 3, session 1, shows exactly this in the platform's export.

C10

Two checks are promised before anyone reads the result

Sample ratio

A fair coin over 60,000 holders gives about 30,000 in each arm. The page states the test and the line: a chi-square test against 50/50, failed below p = 0.001.

Balance

The arms should look alike on traits fixed before the test: channel, holder type, prior lateness, tenure. The page names the traits.

Why promise them in advance? A check that runs only when the result disappoints is not a check. It is a search for an excuse.

Day 3 opens with these two checks on the real export, and one of them fails. Today participants only write the promise. The line of 0.001 for the ratio check is a common convention among experiment teams: stricter than 0.05 because the check runs on every test and a false alarm is costly. Source: Fabijan and others (2019).
